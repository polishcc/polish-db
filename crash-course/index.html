    Polish Survival Crash Course

    10 practical classes for your first days in Poland

    10 classes × 50 minutes

    This crash course is designed for people arriving in Poland for holiday, a short stay, work, study, or to visit Polish family and friends. It focuses on the language you need from day one: reading signs, asking for help, ordering food, finding your way, using transport, shopping, checking in, and handling simple problems.

    What makes this course different?

    • Builds pronunciation systematically: start with vowels and familiar consonants, then move to more challenging Polish sounds.
    • Anchors every lesson in a real-life situation.
    • Introduces grammar only when it becomes useful.
    • Emphasizes patterns and communicative chunks over memorizing tables.
    • Helps learners begin reading Polish immediately and use the language from the first class.

    Course overview

    Class 1 — First contact: reading Polish and asking for help

    Situation: Your first moments in Poland: signs, approaching someone, communication problems, asking where something is, pointing directions

    Phonetics: - vowels: a, e, i, o, u/ó, y, ą, ę - basic word stress

    Grammar: - basic sentence patterns - yes/no questions - simple negation - useful expressions with the verb “to be”

    Class 2 — At a café

    Situation: Ordering a drink or snack in a café

    Phonetics: - pronunciation of ą and ę - voiced/voiceless consonant pairs - final devoicing

    Grammar: - first exposure to the accusative case — biernik - feminine nouns as objects - useful prepositions - with/without expressions leading to instrumental and genitive

    Class 3 - Bus, tram and train

    Situation: Finding the correct bus, tram, train, stop or platform.

    Phonetics: - soft vs hard consonants, the use of letter i to denominate soft consonant - hard vs soft sound contrasts

    Grammar: - yes/no questions - demonstratives - grammatical gender - movement “to” a destination - first exposure to the genitive case — dopełniacz after some prepositions

    Class 4 — Numbers, prices and time

    Situation: Understanding prices, currency exchange rates.

    Phonetics: - the role of i as vowel and softening signal - reading softened consonant combinations

    Grammar: - noun forms after different numbers

    Class 5 — Shopping

    Situation: Buying groceries and everyday items, paying at the till.

    Phonetics:

    Grammar: - quantity of things

    Class 6 — Restaurant Polish

    Situation: Ordering a meal, answering questions, handling dietary needs.

    Phonetics: - consolidation of sound contrasts - phrase stress and rhythm - pronunciation of longer restaurant vocabulary

    Grammar: - accusative (biernik) review - instrumental (narzędnik) - genitive (dopełniacz) for “without” - preposition-dependent noun endings - basic present-tense verbs

    Class 7 — Hotel and accommodation

    Situation: Checking in, asking about breakfast/Wi-Fi, reporting a problem.

    Phonetics: - consonant clusters prz, trz, drz, szcz - breaking long words into sound groups - confident reading of unfamiliar words

    Grammar: - high-frequency present verbs - expressing possession - positive and negative forms - jest / nie ma - infinitives - polite can/may/need expressions - genitive after negation

    Class 8 — Pharmacy, health and emergencies

    Situation: Describing a health problem, asking for a pharmacy or doctor, reporting a loss.

    Phonetics: - connected speech - final devoicing - voicing assimilation - why spoken Polish can sound different from spelling

    Grammar: - describing pain and symptoms - genitive and accusative in common expressions - past tense introduction - masculine and feminine past endings - past forms reflecting speaker gender

    Class 9 — Visiting Polish family and friends

    Situation: Meeting relatives and friends, accepting/refusing hospitality, joining simple conversations.

    Phonetics: - full pronunciation review - oral vowels, ą, ę - voiced/voiceless consonants - hard/soft consonants - sibilant series, i, clusters, stress, connected speech

    Grammar: - formal vs informal address - Pan/Pani vs informal forms - polite questions - conditional forms for polite requests - masculine/feminine forms - review of useful case patterns - review of present and past tense - recognition of common polite native-speaker forms

    Class 10 — Putting it all together

    • Simple conversation in all above situations
    • Reading simple texts
    • Helpful resources: apps, website, etc. to help you while you are in Poland

    By the end of the course

    You should be able to:

    • read unfamiliar Polish words with much greater confidence;
    • understand the main principles of Polish pronunciation;
    • recognise the most important Polish sound contrasts;
    • ask for help when communication becomes difficult;
    • order food and drinks;
    • ask for and understand basic directions;
    • use buses, trams and trains;
    • deal with essential numbers, prices and times;
    • shop and pay;
    • manage basic restaurant and hotel interactions;
    • communicate a simple health or practical problem;
    • participate more comfortably when visiting Polish family and friends;
    • recognise some of the most important Polish grammatical patterns rather than seeing changing word endings as completely unpredictable.

    The emphasis throughout is on practical communication, confident pronunciation and understanding the patterns that make Polish easier to navigate.
